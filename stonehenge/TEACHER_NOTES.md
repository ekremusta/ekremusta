# Significance of Stonehenge: teacher notes

**Learning target (same for every version):** I can explain why Stonehenge is significant, using evidence from the text about how it was built, the landscape around it, and what it meant to people.

## Exit ticket: what a strong answer includes

1. **Building achievement (any two):**
   - It is the only surviving lintelled stone circle.
   - The bluestones came 250 km from the Preseli Hills in Wales; the sarsens came 25 km.
   - The stones were shaped ("dressed") with skill.
   - The stones have interlocking joints that no other prehistoric monument has.
   - It is the most architecturally sophisticated stone circle in the world.
2. **Landscape:**
   - *Name one:* the 350+ burial mounds, the Stonehenge Avenue, the Cursus, Woodhenge, Durrington Walls or Avebury.
   - *Explain one thing it teaches:* ceremonies and burials, contacts with other regions and countries, or how society was organised.
3. **Significance:** a clear claim, supported by evidence from **two different areas**:
   - how it was built (Q1 evidence)
   - its landscape (Q2 evidence)
   - its meaning: a temple aligned with the sun and the solstices; it honoured ancestors and marked the calendar; it was used for 1,500 years (about 100 generations); it is still a place of belief and an icon today.

   A "got it" answer explains *why* the evidence makes Stonehenge important, not just *what* the evidence is.
